## Why AI-Generated Writing Often Feels Artificial

AI-generated text is usually easy to recognize for one reason: it often tries too hard to sound complete.

A sentence may be grammatically correct, logically structured, and professionally worded, yet still feel unnatural. This happens because AI tends to explain more than necessary, repeat the same idea in slightly different forms, and add conclusions even when the point is already clear.

![How to Remove the AI Feel from Your Writing](/uploads/2026/08/8e2f1998-cd3a-4065-be38-5b736c509d82-99abbe65.webp)

For example:

> Through this initiative, we aim to enhance the user experience, strengthen brand influence, and create a mutually beneficial situation for all parties involved.

There is nothing technically wrong with this sentence. The problem is that most of it says very little.

A more natural version would be:

> This initiative focuses on improving the user experience and strengthening the brand, while creating clear benefits for users and partners.

The second version is not better because it sounds more casual. It is better because it gets to the point faster.

## What to Look for Before Rewriting

Before rewriting AI-generated content, go through it sentence by sentence.

Do not immediately replace words or change sentence structures. First identify which parts actually need to be removed, shortened, or merged.

Pay particular attention to the following patterns.

### 1. Sentences That Are Too Perfectly Structured

AI often produces sentences that feel unusually balanced.

They may contain several parallel phrases, similar sentence lengths, or carefully arranged clauses. When every sentence follows the same polished structure, the writing starts to feel mechanical.

Good writing does not need to sound perfectly symmetrical.

If a sentence can express the same information more directly, simplify it.

---

### 2. Repeating the Same Point

AI frequently explains an idea, restates it, and then summarizes it again.

For example:

> The software is designed to improve workflow efficiency. By reducing unnecessary manual operations, users can complete tasks more efficiently. This ultimately leads to a more streamlined and productive workflow.

These three sentences essentially make one point.

A cleaner version would be:

> The software reduces repetitive manual work and helps users complete tasks faster.

The information is still there. The repetition is gone.

---

### 3. Unnecessary Conclusions

AI has a strong tendency to summarize.

A paragraph may already explain a point clearly, but the final sentence often repeats the same message with phrases such as:

* Overall
* In conclusion
* Ultimately
* This demonstrates that
* This makes it an ideal solution
* As a result
* By doing so

These phrases are not automatically bad, but they should only stay when they add something.

If the conclusion simply repeats what the reader already understands, remove it.

---

### 4. Abstract Language

AI often replaces simple ideas with broad business language.

Common examples include:

* enhance value
* empower users
* drive innovation
* strengthen capabilities
* create synergy
* unlock potential
* improve overall efficiency
* achieve sustainable growth
* create a win-win situation

Some of these phrases are useful in the right context. The problem is using them without explaining what actually changes.

Instead of:

> The platform empowers teams to improve collaboration and unlock greater productivity.

Write:

> The platform keeps tasks, comments, and files in one place, so teams spend less time switching between tools.

The second version gives the reader something concrete.

## Remove "Correct but Useless" Sentences

One of the most useful editing rules is simple:

**If removing a sentence does not change the facts, argument, or conclusion, consider deleting it.**

Many AI-generated sentences sound reasonable but contribute almost nothing.

For example:

> In today's rapidly changing digital environment, businesses need to continuously adapt to evolving user expectations.

This statement is difficult to disagree with, but it may not help the reader understand the actual subject.

Unless that context is necessary, start with the real point.

Instead of:

> In today's rapidly changing digital environment, businesses need to continuously adapt to evolving user expectations. Our new platform provides an innovative solution designed to address these challenges.

Write:

> Our new platform reduces the time required to manage customer requests by keeping messages, tasks, and status updates in one workspace.

Specific information is usually more persuasive than broad statements.

---

## Reduce Mechanical Transitions

AI writing often relies heavily on transitions such as:

* Firstly
* Secondly
* Additionally
* Furthermore
* Moreover
* Meanwhile
* Therefore
* Consequently
* In conclusion

These words are useful when they clarify relationships between ideas. They become distracting when every paragraph depends on them.

Good writing often moves naturally from one idea to another without announcing every transition.

For example:

> Furthermore, the software also includes advanced search functionality. Additionally, users can filter results based on multiple criteria.

This can simply become:

> The software also supports advanced search with multiple filters.

Fewer transitions usually make the writing feel more confident.

---

## Avoid Forced Parallel Structure

Another common AI pattern is the three-part list:

> faster, smarter, and more efficient

> simple, intuitive, and powerful

> improve productivity, enhance collaboration, and drive growth

Parallel structures are useful, but AI uses them too frequently.

When every product description contains three benefits and every paragraph ends with three outcomes, the writing becomes predictable.

Keep lists when the items are genuinely distinct. Otherwise, say what matters most.

---

## Do Not Over-Explain Obvious Points

AI tends to explain information that the target audience probably already understands.

For example:

> A search function allows users to enter keywords in order to find relevant files more quickly.

If the audience already understands what search does, this is unnecessary.

A better version might be:

> Search results appear instantly as you type.

That tells the reader something useful about the product.

When editing, ask:

**Does the reader need this explanation, or am I explaining something obvious?**

---

## Preserve Information Density

Removing AI-style writing does not mean making everything casual.

It also does not mean intentionally adding incomplete sentences, slang, spelling mistakes, or conversational filler.

The goal is not to imitate informal human speech.

The goal is to write like someone who knows the subject well enough to distinguish between important information and unnecessary explanation.

Professional writing can still be concise.

Technical writing can still feel natural.

Academic writing can still avoid unnecessary abstraction.

Good editing removes noise without removing substance.

## A Practical Two-Step Editing Method

A reliable workflow is to separate diagnosis from rewriting.

### Step 1: Identify What Should Change

Read the original text sentence by sentence and mark anything that should be:

* deleted
* shortened
* merged
* rewritten more specifically

For each sentence, identify the reason.

Common reasons include:

* repeated information
* no new information
* abstract wording
* excessive explanation
* unnecessary conclusion
* mechanical transition
* template-like phrasing
* too many modifiers

This prevents you from rewriting sentences simply because they "sound AI-generated."

Every change should have a reason.

### Step 2: Rewrite the Text

Once the weak parts are identified, rewrite the content while preserving:

* facts
* arguments
* tone
* technical terminology
* important context
* data
* conclusions supported by evidence

Then remove anything that does not help the reader understand the subject or make a decision.

## A Prompt You Can Use

The following prompt works well for product descriptions, event proposals, press releases, reports, articles, and academic summaries.

```text
Review the text sentence by sentence and identify the parts that sound most like AI-generated writing.

Pay particular attention to:

- overly complete or perfectly structured sentences
- repeated explanations of the same point
- excessive summaries or conclusions
- abstract concepts and vague business language
- statements that sound correct but add no useful information
- mechanical parallel structures
- excessive transition words
- unnecessary explanations of obvious points

First, list the sentences that should be deleted, shortened, merged, or rewritten, and briefly explain why.

Then rewrite the full text while preserving its core facts, arguments, tone, logic, terminology, and important context.

During the rewrite:

1. Remove sentences that do not change the reader's understanding or judgment.
2. Merge repeated ideas.
3. Reduce unnecessary summaries.
4. Replace abstract language with specific information where possible.
5. Reduce mechanical transitions and forced parallel structures.
6. Say directly what can be said directly.
7. Preserve important facts, data, technical terms, and reasoning.

Do not intentionally add spelling mistakes, grammatical errors, slang, or incomplete sentences to make the text appear more human.

Do not reduce information density simply to make the writing more conversational.

The final version should sound like it was written by someone who genuinely understands the subject and knows which details matter.

Use this test when editing:

"If I delete this sentence, does the reader lose any important fact, reasoning, evidence, or context?"

If the answer is no, consider removing it.
```

## The Most Important Editing Rule

The best way to make AI-generated writing feel more natural is not to make it sound more "human."

It is to make every sentence earn its place.

A useful sentence should provide at least one of the following:

* information
* evidence
* reasoning
* context
* judgment

If it does none of these, it probably does not need to be there.

The result is writing that is shorter without feeling thin, professional without sounding robotic, and clear without constantly explaining itself.
